POSSIBILITIES AFRICA
Website Vision & Development Framework
A digital window into the ministry
Leadership + Developer Reference Document
1. Executive Summary
Possibilities Africa (PA) needs a new website that goes beyond a conventional organisational or communication website. The website should present PA as a ministry that understands its work, shows evidence of transformation, shares knowledge, and uses technology to connect people with what is happening across Africa.
The website should take inspiration from large institutional platforms such as the World Bank and United Nations for their structure, authority, knowledge and country-level presentation, while borrowing selected data-visualisation ideas from high-data platforms such as Bloomberg and stock-market portals.
It is important, however, that this remains a WEBSITE—not an internal dashboard or community management system. The PA tracking system remains the operational source of data. The website will consume selected, approved information from that system and present it to the public in a clear, useful and engaging way.
2. The Vision
The proposed website should be a digital window into PA’s ministry.
A visitor should be able to understand who PA is, what PA does, where PA works, how communities are progressing, what PA has learned, and how people can engage or support the work.
The experience should allow a user to move naturally from:
AFRICA  →  COUNTRY  →  COMMUNITY  →  PROGRAM  →  PROGRESS  →  STORY  →  KNOWLEDGE

7. The Data Experience
Data should strengthen the website without taking it over. The public should see meaningful, approved aggregates and trends rather than the full operational detail used by PA staff.
PA at a glance: countries, communities, households, Shalom Groups, projects and other approved indicators.
Growth over time: selected indicators displayed as simple, readable charts.
Geographic view: Africa and country maps showing where PA is working.
Comparisons: selected country, community or programme comparisons where appropriate.
Progress: high-level indicators showing movement through PA’s transformation model.
Data freshness: clearly show when figures were last updated.
Source transparency: where appropriate, explain that figures are drawn from PA’s reporting/tracking data.

10. Technical Architecture for the Developer
The website should sit above the PA data and tracking infrastructure rather than replacing it.
PA INTERNAL SYSTEMS↓DATA / DATABASE LAYER↓API / DATA SERVICES↓PUBLIC PA WEBSITE
The existing PA tracking/community system remains the operational source of truth.
Create or expose APIs for approved public data.
The website consumes only data that has been intentionally approved for public display.
Use a CMS for website content such as stories, news, pages, reports and resources.
Separate operational data from editorial website content.
Design the frontend mobile-first and with low-data usage in mind.
Build reusable components for country pages, community profiles, indicators, charts and stories.
Include strong SEO foundations so PA’s knowledge and country content can be discovered through search engines.
Use role-based access on any administrative/editorial area.
Log data update times and failures so the website does not silently display stale information.

13. Content & Data Governance
PA leadership should approve the public indicators and the level of community information that can be published.
Define who owns website content, who publishes it and who approves sensitive information.
Define the update frequency for each public indicator.
Every dynamic figure should have a clear reporting/update date.
The public website should never expose personal or sensitive operational records.
The data/API layer should be designed so the website can evolve without changing the core tracking system.
14. What Success Looks Like
The project is successful if:
A first-time visitor can understand PA within a few minutes.
Leadership can clearly see the scale and geographic reach of the ministry.
Partners can find credible evidence, stories, reports and progress.
A visitor can move from Africa to a country and, where appropriate, to a community.
PA’s ministry model is clearly explained and consistently represented.
The website is regularly updated rather than becoming a static brochure.
The site is fast, mobile-friendly, searchable and easy to maintain.
The website and PA tracking system work together without becoming the same product.
15. Guiding Principle for the Design & Development Team
“PA’s website should not simply talk about the ministry. It should give people a digital window into the ministry.”
The final experience should combine the institutional clarity of the World Bank and United Nations, the evidence-driven presentation of high-data platforms, and the human stories of PA’s work—while remaining simple enough for ordinary visitors to understand.
16. Basis of This Framework
This framework develops the existing Possibilities Africa Website Concept, particularly its emphasis on data, geography, stories, Africa → Country → Community exploration, knowledge resources, trends and comparisons. The main adjustment is to position those capabilities inside a public institutional website rather than treating the website itself as a dashboard or operational system.
